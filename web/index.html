Brain-Spine Orchestrator

An adaptive, safety-first orchestration layer for the stimulation side of a brain–spine interface. It turns the intention to move into coordinated spatiotemporal stimulation, holds movement quality against fatigue, drift and perturbation in real time — and never leaves a hard safety envelope. The brain is not replaced: control stays voluntary.

2How it works

The orchestration is a small team of specialised components. Each does one job; they hand work to one another; and one of them can stop any of the others.

CPG Orchestrator

The rhythm. Models gait phase-wise (central pattern generator): activation profiles over the gait cycle, left/right shifted half a cycle. Turns intent into coordinated muscle targets.

orchestrator.py

Safety Supervisor

The veto. A single deterministic pass for every command: amplitude / pulse-width / frequency limits, charge per phase and charge density, thermal proxy, an autonomic-dysreflexia watchdog, channel watchdog and a latched e-stop. No ML, absolute veto.

safety.py · config/limits.py

Effectors

The translator. Inverts the recruitment curve to turn muscle targets into concrete stimulation commands (amplitude, pulse-width, frequency) per channel.

effectors.py

Feedback & Adaptation

The correction. Feedback estimates gait state from sensors; the slow Layer-4 controller (a robust integral law, or a trainable policy) compensates fatigue and drift inside the safety envelope.

feedback.py · adaptation.py · learned_adaptation.py

AD Guardian

The life-safety watch. Predicts and prevents autonomic dysreflexia — a dangerous blood-pressure surge — driven by real organ state, tripping on dyssynergic high pressure, silent under coordinated voiding.

ad_guardian.py

Advisor & Commentary (LLM)

The explainers. Between-session guidance and plain-language commentary for clinician and patient. These use language models — and they sit OUTSIDE the real-time loop, by design.

advisor.py · dashboard_agent.py · supervisor_llm.py
Two invariants the whole design rests on. (1) The Safety Supervisor has absolute veto over every single stimulation command — no agent can bypass it. (2) No language model is ever in the real-time motor loop — LLMs advise and explain; they never drive stimulation.

3Why it matters

The real frontier

Walking after spinal-cord injury via a brain–spine interface is already demonstrated (a landmark peer-reviewed study, 2023). The open problem is not making one step — it is adaptive orchestration: holding gait quality against fatigue, drift and balance perturbation, coordinating dozens of muscles, in real time.

Not only walking

The same substrate addresses autonomic function — bladder, bowel and sexual function — which people with SCI rank as high as, or above, walking. That doubles the clinical value of one platform.

Ukraine's unique edge

A large SCI cohort — including a substantial military population — is exactly the data an adaptive layer needs to train and validate, and exactly what the leading labs do not have. This is the resource that lets the approach scale.

Patient + architect

The concept author is both a patient and the engineer of this system — a rare point of trust, lived insight and motivation for a serious partnership.

Honest mock-vs-real

We label what is real and what is a stand-in: intent decoder = stub; stimulator = software model; body = kinematic / Hill muscles / MuJoCo / validated OpenSim; autonomic circuits = reduced physiological models. Nothing here is a medical device — all numbers are illustrative.

Clinician approval

The system never changes stimulation on its own. It PROPOSES a change, proves it is inside the safety envelope, and waits for the clinician to approve, reject or modify it. Every decision is logged — a complete audit trail. This is the «decision support, the clinician decides» principle, made real.

Scheduled autonomic training. Instead of blind catheterization every 4 h, the stimulator runs a timed protocol: every ~4 h it triggers a coordinated low-pressure void (S2 sacral), and over weeks it trains the reflex — capacity rises, residual falls, dysreflexia risk drops. Same idea for bowel (daily) and erectile (on demand). R&D hypothesis grounded in sacral neuromodulation (Brindley/InterStim) + activity-dependent plasticity.
